THE BODY, AND BLURRED BOUNDARIES

Hover to look closer.

A shadow is one of the things we are most familiar with, yet also one of the things we most easily ignore. As long as there is light, and as long as the body stands between the light and a surface, a shadow will naturally appear. From a very young age, we become so used to its presence that we rarely stop to think seriously about what a shadow actually is.

Yet once we begin to observe it more carefully, a shadow is actually full of contradictions. It clearly belongs to me, but it is not my body; it can represent me, yet it contains almost none of the details that truly belong to me; it always follows me, yet it can never fully overlap with me. It is because of this relationship, both close and distant, that the shadow makes me reconsider the seemingly simple question, “Who am I?”

We do not exist in exactly the same way every day, nor do we remain the same in every relationship. If we imagine human identity as a shadow, perhaps that would be closer to reality, because a shadow never pretends that it is permanently stable. It accepts the influence of light, time, position, and environment. It changes every moment, yet it still maintains a connection with the original body.

A mirror lets us see the face; a shadow makes the face disappear. A mirror reflects details; a shadow keeps only the outline. The self in the mirror seems to remain highly similar to the real self, while a shadow can change so much with the light that it may no longer resemble the original body at all.

A shadow cannot exist independently. It must depend on both the body and light. Without a body blocking the light, this particular shadow would not appear. Without light, there would be no shadow to see. The existence of a shadow, therefore, is never independent. It is the result of a relationship.

We do not need to remain exactly the same forever in order to continue being ourselves. What connects the past and the present may not be a face that never changes, but a relationship that continues to exist through constant change. A shadow is the most direct symbol of this relationship. It is always changing, yet always comes from the same body.
